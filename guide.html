World Cup 2026 Team Guide

With 48 teams competing in the expanded 2026 World Cup across the US, Canada and Mexico, giving a full multi-paragraph breakdown for every single country would quickly turn into a massive book.

Instead, here is a quick-fire countdown of the qualified nations, grouped from the heavy tournament favourites down to the historic debutants.

The Elite Favourites

France

The Les Bleus cruised through their qualifying campaign with their usual ruthless efficiency, remaining undefeated and putting on a goal-scoring clinic.

Key Player: Kylian Mbappé

Odds: 9/2

Best Finish: Winners (1998, 2018)

Spain

The reigning European champions have carried their possession-based style into the World Cup cycle and arrive among the favourites.

Key Player: Lamine Yamal

Odds: 9/2

England

The Three Lions topped qualification comfortably and enter the competition with perhaps their strongest squad in years.

Key Player: Harry Kane

Odds: 6/1–7/1

Brazil

Brazil endured a rocky qualification campaign but retain enormous quality and tournament pedigree.

Key Player: Vinícius Júnior

Argentina

The defending world champions remain one of the hardest sides in the world to beat.

Key Player: Lionel Messi

The Heavy Contenders

Portugal, Germany and the Netherlands all arrive with realistic hopes of reaching the latter stages.

The Dangerous Mid-Tier

Italy, Belgium, Uruguay, Colombia and Croatia all possess enough quality and tournament experience to spring surprises.

The Host Nations & Global Changers

The United States, Mexico and Canada benefit from home advantage, while Morocco and Japan continue to rise as global football powers.

The Gritty Longshots

Norway, Austria, Switzerland, Scotland, Türkiye, Senegal, Ghana, Egypt, Ecuador and many others arrive as outsiders capable of causing upsets.

The Historic Debutants

Cape Verde, Curaçao, Jordan and Uzbekistan have all qualified for their first-ever World Cup and will be looking to write their own piece of football history.

ScoreCast24 Verdict: The expanded format makes this World Cup harder to predict than ever. Traditional giants such as France, Spain, England, Brazil and Argentina remain the favourites, but the depth of quality throughout the field means there will almost certainly be several major shocks.
