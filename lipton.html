World Cup History

England’s Other World Cup Win

Long before 1966, a team of County Durham amateurs travelled to Italy and beat some of Europe’s best clubs.

The Sir Thomas Lipton Trophy

The Sir Thomas Lipton Trophy was played in Turin, Italy, in 1909 and 1911. It is often lightheartedly called “the first World Cup”, because it was an early attempt to stage an international football tournament between leading teams from different countries.

Sir Thomas Lipton, the tea magnate, wanted to see a competition between the top clubs of Europe. Teams from Italy, Germany and Switzerland took part, but the English FA refused to send an official representative.

Enter West Auckland

With England unrepresented, West Auckland FC were invited instead. They were not a glamorous professional giant, but a working-class amateur side from County Durham, with several players connected to the local mining community.

Against all expectations, they travelled to Turin in 1909 and won the tournament, beating Stuttgarter Sportfreunde 2–0 in the semi-final before defeating Swiss side FC Winterthur 2–0 in the final.

They Came Back and Won It Again

Two years later, West Auckland returned to Italy to defend the trophy. This time, they beat FC Zürich 2–0 in the semi-final before producing one of the most remarkable early results in football history.

In the 1911 final, West Auckland beat Juventus 6–1. Because they had won the tournament twice, they were awarded the trophy outright.

Why It Matters

It was not the FIFA World Cup as we know it today. It was not contested by national teams, and it was not organised by FIFA. But it was one of the earliest serious attempts to stage a world-style football competition.

For that reason, the Sir Thomas Lipton Trophy has become part of football folklore — especially in County Durham, where West Auckland’s achievement is remembered as England’s forgotten world title.

Fascinating Facts

  • The English FA refused to send a team, so it was up to West Auckland FC, a non-league amateur side, yet they beat teams from some of Europe’s strongest football nations.
  • Their 1911 final opponents, Juventus, would later become one of the biggest clubs in world football.
  • The famous story that West Auckland were invited by mistake because someone confused “W.A.” with Woolwich Arsenal is popular, but modern research suggests it is unlikely.
  • After returning home, financial problems reportedly forced the club to pawn the trophy to a local hotel landlady.
  • The original trophy was later recovered by the village, but was stolen in 1994 and has never been found.
  • A replica trophy is now kept by West Auckland FC.
  • The story was turned into a 1982 TV film called The World Cup: A Captain’s Tale, starring Dennis Waterman.

A Forgotten English Triumph

England’s official World Cup glory came in 1966, but more than half a century earlier, a group of North-East amateurs had already gone abroad and beaten Europe’s best.

Whether you call it the first World Cup, a world club tournament, or just a brilliant football oddity, West Auckland’s Sir Thomas Lipton Trophy wins remain one of the strangest and greatest stories in English football history.
